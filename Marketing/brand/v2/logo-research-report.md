# Own Ultramarine With a Routed Lattice

SkillCo.Work should use a bold, single-color symbol in **ultramarine blue (#2F3CFF)**: a path that runs through a 3x3 grid, draws the letter S, and ends on one lit square. That picture shows what the product does (a prompt lands on exactly one skill) and avoids the orbs, sparkles, asterisks and orange that the AI category already owns. The reasoning rests on four findings. First, iconic tech marks are one owned shape in one flat color, and they earn the right to appear without their name only after years of exposure. Second, the skills-marketplace field is crowded in names and in orange or black, and **a funded edtech company called SkillCo already exists** (Utah, $300K seed in July 2025), so the name itself carries collision risk. Third, blue is the most crowded corporate color, but the saturated "pure" ultramarine zone (hue about 236) was unoccupied among every brand the research checked. It has one technical catch: **#2F3CFF reaches only 2.94:1 contrast on near-black**, so it needs a lighter twin (#7C8BFF, 6.41:1) for dark backgrounds. Fourth, the brand name should be "SkillCo" with "skillco.work" shown small and lowercase as the address, not "SkillCo.Work" as the name. Treat everything below as a shortlist to sketch and test, not a final answer. Several primary sources (OpenAI, Stripe, Hugging Face and Anthropic brand pages, plus most competitor sites) were blocked, no logo was drawn or visually tested, and no trademark search was run.

## Iconic marks win with one owned shape and slow solo use

The strongest marks in the set fall into three families. Pure glyphs (Vercel's triangle, the OpenAI "blossom," Claude's burst) carry the brand with no letters. Character or object marks (GitHub's Invertocat, Hugging Face's emoji face, Notion's cube-shaped N) borrow a memorable thing. Wordmark-led marks (npm's three letters in a red box, Anthropic's custom lettering, Stripe until recently) depend on the name. Vercel's own brand page ships only a black version for light backgrounds and a white version for dark ones, tells designers to "use a symbol in constrained spaces," and sets clear space equal to the symbol's height ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/geist/brands)). GitHub lets the Invertocat stand alone "in GitHub-owned environments or where the brand is already clearly established," and allows it "only in white, black, or in few cases grey or green" ([GitHub Brand](https://brand.github.com/foundations/logo)). Notion uses black and white almost exclusively, with a serif N in a rounded cube ([Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/01/20/notion/)). The pattern is that a mark survives in one color and on both light and dark grounds.

Color-led marks use one flat hue. Amazon's orange smile was drawn by Turner Duckworth in 2000 to link "a" to "z" and suggest the full product range ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/logos-icons/amazon-logo-history)). Its 2021 app icon dropped the wordmark and kept only the smiling arrow ([It's Nice That](https://itsnicethat.com/news/amazon-redesigns-app-icon-graphic-design-260121)), roughly **two decades after the smile was introduced**. Claude's mark is an off-kilter orange starburst, described as deliberately ambiguous and "not a stylized letter C" ([DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/best-designs/logo/claude-logo-design-the-ai-mark-built-to-look-none-of-its-rivals)). npm is red (about #CB3837) and Hugging Face is yellow (#FFD21E, with orange #FF9D00), though those hex codes come from aggregators, not the companies ([BrandColorCode](https://www.brandcolorcode.com/npm); [DesignPieces](https://www.designpieces.com/palette/huggingface-color-palette-hex-and-rgb/)). OpenAI's February 2025 refresh, done by its in-house team with the Berlin type foundry ABC Dinamo, kept the blossom, unified its line weights and added a custom typeface ("OpenAI Sans"), under the brief of "one system, one identity" ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91273217/open-ai-rebrand-chat-gpt-logo); [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/openai-brand-refresh/)).

The lesson for a brand with no recognition yet is that symbol-only use is earned. Stripe is the cautionary case: one report says its 2016 wordmark, with a slanted square for the i-dot, never worked as a standalone logo, and it introduced a separate symbol (a single white diagonal line on a blue square) only in September 2025 ([Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/09/25/extremely-minimal-new-symbol-of-stripe/)). That is a single source I could not confirm on Stripe's own site. Taken together with Amazon and GitHub, it argues for launching with a symbol plus wordmark lockup, then letting the symbol earn solo use. It also matters for SkillCo's design: **a single slanted line on a blue square may look like Stripe's reported new symbol**, which hurts the slash concept discussed below.

Some commonly repeated facts are weaker than they look. The Vercel triangle's "deployment/delta" meaning and the "16px minimum" come from an unsourced blog, and the 16px figure contradicts Vercel's own page, which states no minimum ([DesignYourWay](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/vercel-logo/)). I found no source that measured 16px legibility for any of the ten brands. I found no verified separate ChatGPT logo either: one source describes ChatGPT's mark as six interlocking loops in sage green, another describes an OpenAI black circle called "the point," and I could not tell which is current ([DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/best-designs/logo/claude-logo-design-the-ai-mark-built-to-look-none-of-its-rivals); [ebaqdesign](https://www.ebaqdesign.com/blog/ai-company-branding)). The only ChatGPT-specific element documented is a pulsing "voice disc" ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/openai-brand-refresh/)).

My classification, drawn from the findings above rather than any source, is below.

| Brand | Mark type | Color | Works without its name? |
|---|---|---|---|
| OpenAI | Abstract blossom (plus "point" circle) | Black on aggregator; Creative Review says greys and blues | Increasingly; wordmark being shown more |
| Amazon | Wordmark plus smile arrow | Orange | Yes, after about 20 years |
| Anthropic / Claude | Lettering; orange burst | Terracotta (about #D97757, secondary source) | Burst yes; corporate lettering leans on name |
| Stripe | Wordmark; new diagonal symbol (single source) | Blue-violet (hex unverified) | Only since 2025, if the report is right |
| Vercel | Triangle | Black or white only | Yes |
| Notion | Cube-shaped N | Black and white | Yes |
| GitHub | Invertocat silhouette | Black, white, little grey or green | Only where established |
| Hugging Face | Emoji face | Yellow | Yes |
| npm | Three letters in a box | Red | Depends on letters |

## The skills-marketplace field is crowded in names, and a funded SkillCo already exists

The direct competitors are young and are positioned as leaderboards or command-line registries, not as brands. **Skills.sh launched on 20 January 2026 from Vercel Labs** as "The Open Agent Skills Directory," ranked by install count ([ToolWorthy](https://www.toolworthy.ai/tool/skills-sh)). Smithery hosts MCP servers and skills ([AI Agent Store](https://aiagentstore.ai/ai-agent/smithery)). ClawHub is a free, closed-source registry for publishing and installing skill bundles ([AI Agent Store](https://aiagentstore.ai/ai-agent/clawhub)). A long tail (Forgedemy, Skill Exchange, skills-hub.ai, SkillBazaar.ai, mdskills.ai, agentskill.work) uses Hub, Store, Exchange, Bazaar and Forge naming ([IndieHackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-an-open-marketplace-for-ai-agent-skills-heres-what-i-learned-2ca96753c7)). Since "skill" prefixes and storefront words are everywhere, the name will not differentiate SkillCo, and the visual identity has to.

Color evidence for this field is thin. Only two palettes could be verified: Zapier's orange (#FF4F00 or #FF4A00 depending on the aggregator, with near-black #201515) ([BrandColorCode](https://www.brandcolorcode.com/zapier-logo)) and Hugging Face's yellow and orange. Blue use by the core marketplaces (skills.sh, Smithery, Glama, PulseMCP, mcp.so, the official MCP registry) could not be checked because those sites were blocked. What I can say is that orange and yellow are occupied by Zapier and Hugging Face, and Claude's terracotta adds a third warm occupant. The AI labels themselves mostly avoid blue, and the AI-logo vocabulary is rings, empty centers, radial lines and purple-blue gradients, which commentators call "blanding" ([braindetox.kr](https://braindetox.kr/en/posts/ai_logo_design_convergence_2026.html)). There is a specific danger here: SkillCo sells skills to Claude-style agent harnesses, so **a radial burst or an orange accent would look like a derivative of Anthropic's mark**. The metaphor space of keyways, stamps, index tabs and "certified skill" badges looks open, but that is my inference. Nobody in this research documented which marketplace uses which shape.

The name collision is real. SkillCo (written "Skillco," site skillco.net) is an edtech startup that maps skills students gain from courses to jobs. Its founder and CEO is Steve Lockhart, its founding partner is Utah Valley University, and it reported a **$300K seed round dated 16 July 2025** ([TechBuzz News](https://www.techbuzznews.com/skillco/); [TechBuzz News](https://www.techbuzznews.com/skillco-secures-300k-seed/)). A separate "SKILLCO" team skills-management tool is listed on Appvizer at €129 or €249 a month, and I could not tell whether it is the same company ([Appvizer](https://www.appvizer.es/servicios/centro-formacion/skillco)). No page for skillco.work itself surfaced in search, which is weak evidence. The two businesses differ (education and hiring versus an agent-skill index), so confusion risk is moderate, and trademark risk is unknown because no registry was searched. This is not legal advice. The practical visual rules that follow are to avoid graduation caps, career ladders and "skills passport" imagery, and to make sure first-contact placements show the domain next to the name. I could not find skillco.net's logo or colors, so someone should open it and compare before committing to a palette.

## Ultramarine owns open blue space, but needs a lifted twin on dark

Blue is the safest and most crowded corporate color. About **43% of Fortune 500 logos are blue** (a Canva analysis reported in 2019, methodology unstated) ([Transform](https://www.transformmagazine.net/articles/2019/blue-tops-list-of-most-used-colour-in-fortune-500-logos/)), and a 2026 study of about 2,500 large brands calls blue the largest primary color family, with the most common blues being deep navies such as #004F9F and #0033A0 ([Musemind](https://musemind.agency/blog/most-common-brand-colors-study)). The claim that blue signals trust has weaker support than its reputation. A 2015 review in Frontiers in Psychology found blue linked to perceived quality and trustworthiness "with some null results," called the field "at an early stage," and noted that lightness and saturation are largely neglected ([Elliot 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00368/full)). So the usual shade meanings (sky blue is friendly, electric blue is technical, navy is premium) are design convention, not established science. Treat them as hypotheses.

On the convention, the three options the owner asked about sort out as follows. **Light or sky blue** (hue about 197 to 206) is the most occupied: Dell, Salesforce, HP, Twitter and Intel all sit there. A sky-leaning option like #0090FF is the weakest differentiator. It sits only 35 to 37 RGB-distance units (out of a maximum of 441) from Salesforce, Twitter and Facebook, and its contrast on white is only 3.26:1, which fails the 4.5:1 text standard ([W3C](https://w3.org/WAI/WCAG20/Understanding/contrast-minimum)). It is excellent on dark (5.90:1), which is the mirror image of the problem below. **Navy** is the institutional end (Visa, Ford, Samsung, PayPal, Salesforce navy). It is a good surface color but would make SkillCo look like a bank if used as the brand color. **Electric or royal blue** (hue about 210 to 221) is dense with Facebook, Meta, LinkedIn, IBM, Zoom, Coinbase and Atlassian, per approximate hex values from a third-party aggregator ([abacktools](https://abacktools.com/tools/design/color-tools/famous-brand-color-palettes); [shadcn.io](https://www.shadcn.io/design/ibm/raw)). Beyond that cluster, Stripe (#635BFF, hue about 243) and Linear (a "subtle desaturated blue") hold the indigo zone ([Linear](https://linear.app/brand)).

That leaves a pure ultramarine at hue about 236 and full saturation. **#2F3CFF sits 48 units from Zoom, 50 from IBM and 52 from Coinbase**, near enough to read as "a blue" but not any one of them, and no brand in the set occupies that exact hue at that brightness. The finding is only as strong as the brand list: it covers about 20 brands, many of whose hexes are approximate and Samsung's unsourced. A brand-guideline sweep and a screenshot side by side with these brands would be the real test. I also avoided "International Klein Blue" (about #002FA7, unverified), which is so dark on screen that it reads as navy.

The cost of ultramarine is on dark backgrounds. Blue contributes only about 7% of how bright a color is to the contrast formula, so a fully saturated blue is dark in luminance terms. I re-ran the formula and reproduced every figure below.

| Pairing | Contrast | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| #2F3CFF on white | 6.54:1 | Passes text (4.5:1) |
| White text on #2F3CFF fill | 6.54:1 | Passes; buttons work |
| #2F3CFF on tint #E8EDFF | 5.60:1 | Passes; blue text on pale panels works |
| #2F3CFF on #0A0E1A (near-black) | 2.94:1 | Fails even the 3:1 icon standard |
| #2F3CFF on #0B1230 (brand navy) | 2.81:1 | Fails |
| Lifted #7C8BFF on #0A0E1A | 6.41:1 | Passes; use for links, thin icons, glow on dark |
| #6B7BFF on #0A0E1A | 5.41:1 | Passes |
| #5B6CFF on #0A0E1A / #0B1230 | 4.62 / 4.40:1 | Passes on one, fails on the other |
| #E8EDFF (tint-white mark) on #0A0E1A | 16.49:1 | Safest mark color on dark |
| Lime #C6FF3D on #0A0E1A | 16.30:1 | Excellent accent on dark |
| Lime #C6FF3D on #2F3CFF | 5.53:1 | Readable on the brand blue |
| Lime on white | 1.18:1 | Fails as text; use only as a fill with dark ink |
| #0090FF (sky alternative) on white / near-black | 3.26 / 5.90:1 | Fails on light, passes on dark |

So the mark needs two blues: base ultramarine for light backgrounds and large filled areas, and a lifted blue or tinted white for anything thin on dark. One restrained accent is the usual pattern across the brands examined (Visa's gold on navy, Stripe's cyan, Linear and IBM with none). Lime #C6FF3D is the strongest candidate on contrast and is not a color I found any competitor pairing with blue, though I did not check lime systematically. Cyan as a second color is out because Stripe and Salesforce already pair blue with it. Dark-first is my judgment, not a surveyed fact. I found no 2026 developer preference data. Lead with a dark navy field for hero, social and video, where ultramarine glows, and use light pages for the catalog and documentation, where #2F3CFF text reads best.

## Name it SkillCo, and show skillco.work as the address

The naming evidence points against putting the domain in the name, with one tension. Brands that keep the TLD do so because the bare word is generic and cannot be trademarked (Booking.com, where the Supreme Court held that ".com" gave the name a protectable identity) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booking.com)), because the dotted form is the joke (Del.icio.us), or because the brand is young and the URL is the call to action (1990s Amazon). Established brands drop it. Notion moved from notion.so to notion.com ([Create With](https://www.createwith.com/tool/notion/updates/notion-moves-to-notioncom-after-years-long-domain-acquisition)), and Perplexity, Mistral AI, Replit and Cursor use the clean name in copy and keep the TLD in the URL ([Wikipedia: Perplexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perplexity_AI); [Wikipedia: Mistral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_AI); [Wikipedia: Replit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replit)). When Amazon dropped ".com" is disputed: Creative Bloq says 2000, while Marketplace quotes the designer saying the dot-com was removed because it was "a limitation that doesn't apply to Amazon anymore," in a 2012 context ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/logos-icons/amazon-logo-history); [Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/2019/07/05/how-amazons-logos-reflect-its-evolution)). Even dotted names get shortened in practice, as with Character.ai becoming "c.ai" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Character.ai)).

The practical reasons for a clean name are strong. A symbol can stand for "SkillCo" with an S, but not for "SkillCo.Work," which would need a W it cannot carry. If the domain ever changes, a brand named SkillCo survives and one named SkillCo.Work must be renamed. The domain itself is also the weaker link: **.work costs $11.25 a year** at Vercel's registrar (checked live on 2026-10-15) but carries a reputation overhang. Krebs reported in 2018 that .work was among the "riskiest and spammiest" new TLDs, though a recent (Dec 2025 to Feb 2026) spam report does not list it in its top 20, which is absence of evidence, not a clean bill ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2018/06/bad-men-at-work-please-dont-click/); [Cybercrime Information Center](https://www.cybercrimeinfocenter.org/spam-activity-quarter-over-quarter-tlds-december-february-2026)). Live lookups also show skillco.com and skillco.ai are already registered by others, while **skillco.dev and skillco.io were available**. The .io extension carries sovereignty-transition uncertainty tied to the Chagos Islands ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.io)), which makes .dev the lower-risk defensive purchase. I could not confirm whether Pat's account owns skillco.work.

The tension is that "SkillCo" alone is the exact string the edtech company uses. My resolution is a lockup in which the clean name is the headline and the domain always appears small, lowercase and in a monospace font directly beneath or beside it in first-contact settings (video end cards, site header, social bios, QR placements). The domain then does the distinguishing work without becoming the name. For per-skill pages, the skill is the headline and SkillCo is the endorsement ("n8n Engineer on SkillCo"), with the full subdomain shown as a mono chip. On search, Google's John Mueller said in 2018 that subdomains are sometimes treated as part of the main site and sometimes not, so if each skill page needs to rank, the canonical content should sit at skillco.work/skills/n8n-engineer with the pretty subdomain redirecting to it ([Bruce Clay](https://www.bruceclay.com/blog/subdomains-vs-subdirectories-seo/)). As a hedge, send marketing email from a stronger-reputation domain, not skillco.work.

"Work" is better used as language than as part of the name. "Skills that work" is a natural line, but the word is polysemous and the .work registry itself positioned the TLD around jobs and employment ([ICANNWiki](https://icannwiki.com/.work)), which risks making a skill index look like a job board. The existing tagline "Turn Skills Into Companies" describes the seller story. It does not describe the index-and-route story the brand is growing into, so test a line like "Skills that work" before retiring it.

## A 2026 mark must read at 16 pixels and double as a video frame

The 2026 consensus is that a logo is a system that behaves, not one static lockup. LogoLounge's 2026 report, covering more than 30,000 logos, calls motion "a big overarching theme" ([The Dieline](https://thedieline.com/logoloung-releases-its-2026-trend-report-and-everything-is-on-the-move/)). Others describe "responsive logo systems" sharing one core idea across sizes ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/logos-icons/these-logo-design-trends-will-define-2026)) and kinetic branding where the static logo is the "hero frame" that every animation lands on ([WeAndTheColor](https://weandthecolor.com/best-logo-design-trends-of-2026-whats-working-whats-tired-and-whats-next/209969)). For SkillCo's walkthrough videos and per-skill pages, that means designing one static mark and deriving the build, draw and loop animations from it.

For small sizes, the advice is consistent: simpler shape, bolder strokes, no baked-in effects. Apple's WWDC25 icon guidance specifies a 1024px canvas, flat frontal artwork, "avoid sharp edges and thin lines," and no baked-in shadows or bevels, with five appearance modes (default, dark, clear/monochrome, dark tint, light tint) ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/220)). A favicon set needs an SVG plus a 32x32 ICO or PNG fallback and a 180x180 apple-touch icon, because Safari does not support SVG favicons (the source estimates about 20% of users), so the 16px version must be checked by hand, not auto-exported ([favicon.im](https://favicon.im/blog/svg-favicon-complete-guide)). Mistral's brand page ships four logo versions (gradient, inverted, black, white) and forbids stretching, rotating or recoloring ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/brand)). SkillCo should ship at least full-color, one-color, reversed (white or tint on dark) and an app-icon glyph on a rounded square. The classic Paul Rand-derived checks apply as written: pass a black-and-white test, shrink-then-enlarge test and a draw-it-in-ten-seconds test ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/how-good-is-your-logo)).

For animation, one guide (a secondary blog) gives usable numbers: loaders under 2 seconds and looping, reveals under 1.5 seconds, intros up to 3 seconds, Lottie files under about 50 KB, and "draw-on" tracing only for marks with real line geometry, since solid fills drawn on look like render errors ([brainy.ink](https://brainy.ink/paper/logo-animation-guide)). It also says to design for silence first because many brand animations play muted. That creates a trade-off: a stroke-based master animates well, but thin strokes fail at 16px. The solution is a heavy-stroke master with a solid expanded version for small sizes. Video adds one more constraint for a saturated blue: chroma subsampling softens color edges, so use large uniform ultramarine fields and bold shapes, render titles in white or tint, and export masters at the highest chroma fidelity available ([RED](https://www.reddigitalcinema.com/red-101/video-chroma-subsampling)). Dark gradients can band in 8-bit video, but that is general practice and not sourced here. No source gave a standard sonic-logo length; a 1 to 2 second stinger synced to the "lock" moment is my inference. Provide a static hero frame as the reduced-motion fallback.

## Grids, slashes and index tabs beat orbs, sparkles and keys

The product's differentiator is determinism: a fixed schema where a prompt resolves to exactly one cell. Metaphors that show "grid, one lit point, a path" tell that story. Orbs, sparkles and asterisks say only "AI." The AI category's default shapes are well documented: Claude's starburst, Perplexity's asterisk (animated as a spinning cursor), Gemini's sparkle, Grok's ring, and rings and empty centers generally ([ebaqdesign](https://www.ebaqdesign.com/news/perplexity-rebrand); [braindetox.kr](https://braindetox.kr/en/posts/ai_logo_design_convergence_2026.html)). Mistral claims a retro pixel identity, "a pixelated, striped M with a hidden cat face" ([ebaqdesign](https://www.ebaqdesign.com/blog/ai-company-branding)), so a grid-of-dots S must be built differently: a drawn path through nodes, not a bitmap of a letter. Most "who else uses it" attributions below are my background knowledge and were not checked, so treat the verdicts as judgments.

| Metaphor | What it says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Grid / lattice (3x3) | Schema, order, a place for everything; one cell lights up | **Strong** if the lit-cell-plus-path rule is unique |
| Slash `/` | The character that invokes a skill, also a route | Strong in concept, but a single diagonal on a blue tile risks resembling Stripe's reported symbol |
| Notch / squared C | C for SkillCo, socket, a prompt docking into a skill | Risky unless squared and off-center; a round ring joins the AI "orb" family and a symmetric notch reads as a power icon |
| Stepped S (stairs, stacked pages) | S, levels and layers at once | Risky-to-strong; solid and small-size friendly, but uniform offsets look like a generic stack |
| Index tab / lit card | Index, library, a selected entry | Strong as a secondary glyph for per-skill pages |
| Key or keyhole | Access | Risky; reads as login or security |
| Node graph, constellation | Connectivity, discovery | Overused; mushy at 16px, fine as a video pattern |
| Plug / connector | Install a skill into a harness | Risky-to-strong if abstracted; literal plugs feel dated |
| Blocks, bricks, stacked layers | Modularity | Overused |
| Brackets `{ }`, cursor `>_` | Code, terminal | Overused alone; good as animation behavior (blinking loader) |
| SC monogram, S+C ligature | Initials | Risky; generic, or fragile at small size |
| Badge / seal / shield | Verification, billing trust | Risky as the main mark; good as a secondary "verified skill" sticker |
| Compass / crosshair | Precision matching | Risky; collides with ring sameness and targeting tone |

Billing is the hardest idea to draw, so leave it out of the symbol and express "routed onward to billing" in motion, where the lit cell hands off to a receipt line in the product film.

## Recommendation: ultramarine, a routed-lattice S, and a SkillCo lockup

**Color.** Make ultramarine #2F3CFF the brand blue for light backgrounds, buttons and large fields. Use #7C8BFF (or the tinted white #E8EDFF for the mark itself) on dark, ink #0A0E1A or navy #0B1230 for surfaces, off-white #F6F7FB for pages, and lime #C6FF3D as the single accent, used once or twice per screen as a fill with dark ink. This replaces the older BRAND.md dark and violet palette. Violet also sits inside the purple-blue range the AI category already overuses. I did not evaluate the old gear-S mark in this research, so I make no claim about it beyond its being a gear (a generic metaphor I did not find sourced) and being tied to an S-only name.

**Symbol.** Draw the mark as a heavy-stroke path through a 3x3 grid that forms an S, with the end node lit in the accent. Keep it to one or two primitives (square nodes or dots, one line weight, one corner radius), no text inside, and build it on a fixed grid and angle system. At 16 to 24px, show only the path and the lit end node. On a dark app tile, use the tinted-white version. Avoid circles, sparkles, asterisks and starbursts.

**Lockup.** Icon plus a custom or carefully chosen "SkillCo" wordmark, with "skillco.work" in a small lowercase mono font (BRAND.md already specifies JetBrains Mono) as a secondary element. A wordmark with a clever detail does not automatically yield a standalone symbol (the Stripe case), so the name must carry recognition at launch.

**Ranked concept directions.**

| Rank | Direction | Construction | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Routed-lattice S | 3x3 nodes joined by one continuous path forming an S; end node in the accent | Could read as a pixel S like Mistral's; the path and lit end must be visibly different |
| 2 | Stepped S | Two offset heavy bars or steps forming an S; solid, monochrome-ready | Resembles a generic stack icon if offsets are uniform |
| 3 | Squared notch-and-dock C | Heavy rounded-square ring, one off-center notch, one accent dot docking into it | Joins the ring family; reads as a power icon if symmetric |
| 4 | Slash mark | One bold diagonal with a distinctive cap and accent end, not on a plain blue tile | Resembles Stripe's reported symbol; very generic |
| 5 | Lit-cell index tab | Grid with one raised tab or card; each skill lights a different cell | Not strong enough alone; best as a per-skill system glyph |

The 3x3 lattice also works as a background pattern for product pages and videos, with each skill's page lighting a different cell, which gives n8n-engineer.skillco.work and every other page one shared mark with distinct states. The ranking is my judgment from the evidence above, not a tested result. Sketching all five in pure black and white at 16, 24 and 48px, then asking a few strangers to redraw each after five seconds, is a one-day test that would settle it.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which blue?" to "which blue can still be seen on a black screen?" Most of the crowded blues are dark in luminance terms, and the open ultramarine territory is open partly because it is hard to use: it fails on near-black and needs a lifted twin. That makes the dark-mode version of the mark, in tinted white with a lime end node, the real hero asset for videos and social, with the base blue reserved for light pages and filled shapes. The second shift is that the strongest candidate for the symbol is also the strongest explanation of the product. A path through a grid ending on one lit cell is the deterministic match-and-route in a single image, which no orb or sparkle can claim.

The open risks are about the name and the evidence, not the drawing. A funded SkillCo already holds skillco.net, and skillco.com and skillco.ai are gone, so a trademark search should come before any money goes into the final mark, and the domain chip in the lockup is doing real work as a differentiator. The cheapest next steps are a one-day sketch test of the five concepts, a screenshot sweep of competitor favicons and skillco.net, and a trademark check, in that order, before committing to artwork and animation.
